# Prior art for medication-adherence tracking and patient "Care / engagement / health score" features (state as of October 2026)

Researcher note on method and reliability: research run on 2026-10-04. The web search tool was rate-limited for part of the session and PubMed Central / BMJ Open / JMIR full-text pages blocked automated fetching (captcha / 403 / empty body). Findings below are therefore drawn mainly from search-result extracts of the cited pages, not from full-text reading, unless stated. Anything I could not source in this session is placed under "Gaps" and explicitly labelled as unverified. Nothing in "Cited Findings" is from memory.

## 1. What do medication adherence apps offer, and do any share adherence data with the prescribing doctor or a caregiver?

### Takeaway
Dose logging as taken/skipped, reminders, adherence history and report export are commodity features in Medisafe, MyTherapy, Apple Health and Eka Care; caregiver visibility (Medisafe "Medfriend", Apple Health Sharing) and doctor-facing PDF reports already exist. MediBridge's medicine tracker and 14-day adherence chart are therefore not novel; the only structural difference is that MediBridge's medicines can originate from the doctor's own prescription inside the same system.

### Cited Findings
- Medisafe "Medfriend": a designated caregiver can "view the person's medications and adherence" and is notified only if a dose is missed (alert sent 1 hour after scheduled time if not marked within 30 minutes). Medfriends cannot mark doses or add/change medications. — [Medisafe Help Center, caregiver guide](https://help-center.medisafe.com/en/articles/11793638-caregiver-guide-how-to-use-medisafe-to-manage-a-family-member-s-medications)
- Medisafe (company-reported, not peer-reviewed): 40% of previously non-adherent users (taking medications less than 80% of the time) became adherent (at least 80%) after adding a Medfriend. Note that 80% is the same adherence cut-off used by PQA/CMS. — [Medisafe, "The impact of Medisafe's Medfriend caregiver feature on adherence"](https://medisafe.com/education-resources/the-impact-of-medisafes-medfriend-caregiver-feature-on-adherence)
- Medisafe is described by a secondary source as able to share adherence reports with doctors via PDF and to share reports with family members or healthcare providers. This is a third-party description; the Medisafe help page I fetched did not itself describe the doctor-report mechanism. — [WomenTech, "Medisafe medication management made easy"](https://www.womentech.net/how-to/medisafe-medication-management-made-easy)
- Medisafe's own site markets a "For Providers" offering to "support patients between visits with digital tools that reinforce treatment plans and medication use" (no detail of a doctor dashboard on the page fetched). — [Medisafe](https://www.medisafe.com/?p=1513)
- The Medisafe app as tested in the MedISAFE-BP trial "includes reminder alerts, adherence reports, and optional peer support". — [PubMed 29710289, Morawski et al., JAMA Intern Med 2018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29710289/)
- MyTherapy (smartpatient, Germany): reminders for all medications, a pill tracker with a logbook for both skipped and confirmed intakes, refill reminders, measurements (weight, blood pressure, blood sugar), mood tracker and health journal; a health report is available in-app and as a PDF "for discussion with your doctor or pharmacist". Free, no registration required. — [AlternativeTo listing for MyTherapy](https://alternativeto.net/software/mytherapy/about/); [MyTherapy diabetes page](https://www.mytherapyapp.com/diabetes-blood-glucose-app)
- Apple Health Medications (iOS/iPadOS/watchOS): user logs each dose as "Taken" or "Skipped"; can tap a medication to review how frequently it was taken or skipped; "Export PDF" saves or shares the current medication list; if the user shares health data with family and friends, medications can be included. — [Apple Support 105064, "Add and log medications with iPhone, iPad, and Apple Watch"](https://support.apple.com/en-ca/105064)
- India: Eka Care (app-store description claims "over 15 million Indians") includes a Medication Tracker with reminders "for better adherence", ABHA creation, storage/sharing of ABHA-linked records (reports, prescriptions, diagnoses), vitals trackers (BP, glucose, cholesterol, BMI, sleep, period), sync with Health Connect / Google Fit, and an AI HealthBot. Eka also sells a separate doctor-side product ("Eka Doc: AI, Scribe, EMR/EHR"). — [Apple App Store, "Eka: Health AI, ABHA, Records"](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1561621558); [Apple App Store, Eka Doc](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1598859349)

### Inferences
- MediBridge's three dose states (taken / missed / skipped) are a superset of Apple's two (taken / skipped) and equivalent to Medisafe/MyTherapy logbooks; this is not a differentiator.
- Existing consumer apps push adherence data to clinicians mostly as an exported PDF that the patient chooses to send, or to a lay caregiver. A doctor seeing, inside their own consultation screen, live adherence to the prescription they themselves wrote is a workflow that consumer adherence apps do not natively provide because they are not the prescribing system. MediBridge already holds prescription, appointment and dose-log data in one database, so it could do this; today it does not (Care Score and adherence are patient-only).
- The Eka Care pair (patient app + Eka Doc EMR) is the closest India-specific analogue to MediBridge's patient+doctor architecture, and is the product evaluators are most likely to have in mind.

### Gaps
- Not researched in this session because of rate limits (no sources retrieved; treat any statement about them as unverified): Dosecast, CareClinic, Mango Health, Round Health, generic "Pill Reminder" apps, Google Health / Fitbit medication tracking, Samsung Health medication tracking. From background knowledge only (UNVERIFIED): Mango Health was known for points/rewards gamification of adherence and is understood to have been discontinued; Round Health was a minimalist iOS reminder app; Samsung Health added medication tracking in 2023-24. The report writer should not cite these without verification.
- I could not confirm from a primary Medisafe page exactly what format the doctor report takes or whether a clinician dashboard exists outside pharma-sponsored programs.
- Could not verify whether Eka Doc surfaces the patient's reminder-adherence data to the doctor.

## 2. What existing composite scores resemble the Care Score?

### Takeaway
Scores on a 0-100 scale that summarise patient engagement (PAM), medication adherence (PDC with an 80% threshold), overdue care (Epic care gaps / health maintenance) and wellness (dacadoo, Oura, WHOOP, Aditya Birla Healthy Heart Score) all exist, so "a 0-100 health/engagement number with a breakdown" is established prior art. They differ from the Care Score in their inputs: none of those found combines dose-log adherence, checkup recency and follow-up completion in one patient-facing number.

### Cited Findings
- Patient Activation Measure (PAM-13, Insignia Health, now distributed via Phreesia): 13-item survey, score 0-100, four levels: Level 1 "Disengaged and Overwhelmed", Level 2 "Becoming Aware but Still Struggling", Level 3 "Taking Action and Gaining Control", Level 4 "Maintaining Behaviors and Pushing Further". Cut-offs: below 47.1 = level 1; 47.1-53.2 = level 2; 53.3-70.2 = level 3; above 70.2 = level 4. — [Insignia Health PAM](https://www.insigniahealth.com/?p=2526); [Phreesia PAM FAQ (PDF)](https://memberconnect.phreesia.com/rs/753-LZD-147/images/PAM_FAQ_8ffb939234.pdf); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03699748](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03699748)
- PAM is used as a formal performance measure ("Gains in Patient Activation (PAM) Scores at 12 Months"). — [American College of Physicians performance measures](https://www.acponline.org/clinical-information/performance-measures/gains-in-patient-activation-pam-scores-at-12-months)
- PAM evidence (2013): patients with the lowest activation had predicted costs 8% higher in the base year and 21% higher in the first half of the next year than the most activated patients (Fairview Health Services, Minnesota). — [Hibbard, Greene, Overton, Health Affairs 2013](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/epdf/10.1377/hlthaff.2012.1064); [Commonwealth Fund summary](https://www.commonwealthfund.org/publications/journal-article/2013/feb/patients-lower-activation-associated-higher-costs-delivery)
- PAM criticism: it is a licensed commercial instrument, not free; a National Quality Forum standing committee "expressed concerns about the proprietary nature of the Patient Activation Measure (PAM) and associated algorithms". — [NQF Person and Family Centered Care off-cycle draft report](https://www.qualityforum.org/Projects/n-r/Person_and_Family_Centered_Care/Off-Cycle_Draft_Report_for_Voting.aspx); [Insignia Health licence page](https://www.insigniahealth.com/license/); [Health Innovation Forum](https://www.healthinnovationforum.org/hif-news/the-patient-activation-measure/)
- Proportion of Days Covered (PDC): (days covered) / (days in period) x 100, computed from pharmacy refill claims; CMS treats a member as adherent at PDC of 80% or higher. — [Outcomes knowledge base on PDC](https://outcomes.com/knowledge-base/proportion-of-days-covered-pdc-in-outcomes); [PQA PDC rationale (PDF)](https://www.pqaalliance.org/assets/docs/PQA_PDC-CMP_Rationale.pdf)
- Five PQA measures were in the 2025 Medicare Part D Star Ratings: Medication Adherence for Diabetes Medications, for Hypertension (RAS antagonists), for Cholesterol (Statins), MTM Program Completion Rate for CMR, and Statin Use in Persons with Diabetes. (2025 rating year; I did not retrieve the 2026/2027 list.) — [PQA, "PQA Measure Use in CMS' Part D Quality Programs"](https://www.pqaalliance.org/medicare-part-d)
- Epic "Care Gaps" / "Health Maintenance": clinical decision support that matches patient characteristics to evidence-based preventive or chronic-disease guidelines and recommends actions; shown to clinicians in the chart and to patients in MyChart, where a patient needing a preventive test "will receive a red flag alert in the Health Reminders section". — [Alberta Health Services Connect Care FAQ](https://questions.connect-care.ca/2022/10/28.html); [Premier Health, 2013](https://www.premierhealth.com/news-and-events/news/2013/01/29/dr-davis-shares-how-mychart-electronic-medical-records-play-a-role-in-preventive-care)
- A registered trial uses Epic Health Maintenance status to send pre- and post-visit text nudges about overdue care gaps and to auto-pend orders (mammogram, DEXA, HbA1c, metabolic panel, urine microalbumin, lipids, hepatitis C). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07636187](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07636187)
- dacadoo Health Score: a number from 0 to 1,000 built from biometric, lifestyle and psychometric inputs across Body, Mind and Lifestyle; moves in near real time; vendor states it is based on "400 million person-years" of data; sold to insurers/businesses. — [dacadoo Health Score](https://dacadoo.com/solutions/dhep/health-score)
- Oura: Readiness, Activity and Sleep Scores and their contributors are each rated 0-100; Readiness uses nine contributors (resting heart rate, HRV balance, body temperature, recovery index, sleep, sleep balance, sleep regularity, previous-day activity, activity balance). This is a direct precedent for "0-100 score plus per-contributor breakdown" as a UI pattern. — [Oura support](https://support.ouraring.com/hc/en-us/articles/360057791533); [Oura blog, Readiness Score](https://ouraring.com/blog/readiness-score/)
- WHOOP Recovery: 0-100% from HRV, resting heart rate, respiratory rate and sleep obtained versus sleep needed (secondary source). — [AthleteData, Oura vs WHOOP](https://www.athletedata.health/guides/oura-vs-whoop)
- India, Aditya Birla Health Insurance: "Healthy Heart Score" computed from a health assessment (height/weight, BMI, smoking, hip-to-waist ratio, blood sugar, cholesterol), compared with peers of same age/gender, colour-banded; "Active Dayz" earned by 10,000 steps, 300 calories burned or a 30-minute session at a partner gym; the two together determine "HealthReturns" (up to 30% of premium back with 13 Active Dayz per month + green score; up to 100% with 325+ Active Dayz per year + green score). — [Aditya Birla Healthy Heart Score](https://adityabirlacapital.com/healthinsurance/wellness-and-rewards/healthy-heart-score); [Aditya Birla HealthReturns](https://adityabirlacapital.com/healthinsurance/wellness-and-rewards/health-returns); [Ditto explainer](https://joinditto.in/articles/health-insurance/aditya-birla-health-insurance-healthreturns/)

### How each resembles / differs from MediBridge's Care Score
| Prior art | Same as Care Score | Different from Care Score |
|---|---|---|
| PAM-13 | 0-100 engagement-style number with levels; shown to clinicians to tailor care | Derived from a 13-question attitude/confidence survey, not from behaviour logs; proprietary and licensed; primarily clinician/organisation-facing |
| PDC / Star Ratings | Adherence percentage over a window with a threshold (80%) | From pharmacy refill claims, not self-logged doses; reported at plan/pharmacy level to payers, not shown to patients as a score; medication only |
| Epic Care Gaps / MyChart Health Reminders | Tracks overdue checkups and recommended follow-ups; visible to both patient and clinician | A list of overdue items, not a single weighted number; guideline-driven rather than doctor-recommendation-driven; no dose-level adherence |
| dacadoo, Oura, WHOOP, Healthy Heart Score | Single number with contributor breakdown; gamified/incentivised | Inputs are biometrics, activity, sleep and lifestyle; measure health status or readiness, not adherence to a care plan |
| Medisafe / MyTherapy adherence % | Dose-log adherence percentage and charts | Single ingredient only; no visit or follow-up component |

### Inferences
- The Care Score's individual ingredients each map onto an established construct: 40% medication component = self-reported analogue of PDC; 35% checkup-recency + 25% follow-up completion = a simplified version of Epic-style care gaps / HEDIS-style follow-up measures. What is new is only the particular weighted blend and its presentation to a patient, not the idea.
- The Care Score measures engagement with care processes, not health. Naming and UI should make that explicit, since dacadoo/Oura-type "health scores" imply physiological status and a patient with a high Care Score can still be unwell.
- The 40/35/25 weights are arbitrary and unvalidated, whereas PAM is psychometrically validated and PDC's 80% threshold is an industry standard. An evaluator can fairly call the Care Score an unvalidated heuristic; anchoring the medication component to the recognised 80% threshold would be a cheap way to borrow legitimacy.

### Gaps
- Not researched (rate limits; no sources retrieved): Medication Possession Ratio (MPR) definition, NCQA HEDIS measures (for example follow-up-after-hospitalisation measures), Epic proprietary risk scores, Fitbit Daily Readiness, Apple Vitals, Vitality/Discovery points and Vitality Age, HealthifyMe, Hello Heart, Lark. UNVERIFIED background: MPR is the older refill-based ratio that can exceed 100% and PDC is preferred by PQA; Discovery Vitality uses points and status tiers rather than a 0-100 score. Verify before citing.
- I did not retrieve the list of PQA adherence measures for the 2026 or 2027 Star Ratings, nor confirm reported plans by CMS to change the weighting or risk-adjust the adherence measures.
- No source found describing whether Epic exposes any single numeric "engagement score" to patients in MyChart.

## 3. Does any product compute a score from the same three ingredients (adherence + visit recency + follow-up completion) and show it to patients, doctors and administrators together?

### Takeaway
I found no shipping product that publishes a single score built from exactly dose-log adherence + recency of last checkup + completion of doctor-recommended follow-ups and exposes it to patient, doctor and administrator. However, composite indices combining medication adherence with appointment-keeping exist in research and in a US patent, so the concept is at most an incremental recombination, and absence of evidence here reflects a limited search, not a proof of novelty.

### Cited Findings
- A clinical study used a three-point composite adherence score awarding one point each for: more than 80% of prescribed medications taken over 30 days, more than 80% of appointments kept during the past six months, and 100% of activity log books kept. This is structurally close to the Care Score (30-day medication window + appointment attendance + task completion). — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT04029298, "Home-based Prediabetes Care in Acoma Pueblo"](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04029298) (attribution to this specific record is from a search extract; the writer should open the record to confirm the wording)
- US Patent 11,036,831, "Systems, methods, and apparatuses for combining medication adherence and diagnostic lab scores to determine intervention": an "adherence manager" may combine an adherence score, a lab value score and/or a patient response score into a single combined score to decide on interventions. — [USPTO patent 11036831 (PDF)](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11036831)
- Adherence research routinely treats pill counts, refill records, sessions attended, appointments kept and log books kept as joint indicators of adherence. — [PMC4926000, table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4926000/table/T2)
- FICO built a "Medication Adherence Score" that predicts which patients are likely to skip medication using public data such as employment status, age and gender (a predictive risk score for payers, not a patient-facing behaviour score). — [Better Health (getbetterhealth.com) adherence tag](https://getbetterhealth.com/tag/adherence)
- Epic Care Gaps are visible to both the clinician (Health Maintenance activity) and the patient (MyChart Health Reminders), showing that dual patient/clinician visibility of overdue checkups and follow-ups is established. — [Connect Care FAQ](https://questions.connect-care.ca/2022/10/28.html); [Premier Health, 2013](https://www.premierhealth.com/news-and-events/news/2013/01/29/dr-davis-shares-how-mychart-electronic-medical-records-play-a-role-in-preventive-care)

### Inferences
- Defensible novelty claim: "a transparent, patient-visible composite of three care-process behaviours, computed inside the same system the doctor prescribes in". Indefensible claim: "the first health/engagement score" or "the first adherence score".
- MediBridge currently fails the "shown to patients, doctors and administrators together" test itself, since only the patient sees the score. The differentiating step that prior art leaves open is closing the loop: doctor sees the breakdown and the specific missed follow-ups at consultation time; admin sees aggregate, de-identified distributions (the role PDC/Star Ratings play for payers).
- Exposing an individual score to an admin raises privacy and consent issues that ABDM's consent-manager model handles explicitly (see section 5); aggregate-only admin views avoid this.

### Gaps
- No patent search beyond one incidental hit; no search of Indian patents or of Google Play / App Store listings specifically for "care score". A targeted patent and app-store search is needed before making any novelty claim in writing.
- Could not verify whether Indian platforms (Practo, Eka Care, Apollo 24|7, Tata 1mg, HealthifyMe) show any composite engagement score.

## 4. What does published research say about whether such scores and reminders improve adherence or outcomes, and what are the criticisms?

### Takeaway
Apps roughly double the odds of self-reported adherence in pooled RCTs, but the evidence is mostly self-report, short-term and small, and the best trial of a market-leading app (Medisafe) improved self-reported adherence slightly with no change in blood pressure. The Cochrane review of 182 RCTs concludes that adherence interventions in general are "mostly complex and not very effective".

### Cited Findings
- Armitage et al., BMJ Open 2020 (searches to November 2018): 9 RCTs, 1,159 participants; app users were more likely to self-report adherence, OR 2.120 (95% CI 1.635 to 2.747, n=988); conditions included cardiovascular disease, depression, Parkinson's disease, psoriasis and multimorbidity; meta-regression of behaviour change techniques found no significant association with effect size. — [BMJ Open 2020;10:e032045](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/10/1/e032045); [PMC7045248](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7045248)
- JMIR 2025 systematic review and meta-analysis (apps for chronic conditions): 14 RCTs published 2014-2022, sample sizes 57 to 412; meta-analyses grouped by Morisky scales and percentage-adherence scales showed app-based interventions improved medication adherence. Pooled effect sizes not retrieved (full text blocked). — [J Med Internet Res 2025, e60822](https://www.jmir.org/2025/1/e60822/PDF); [PMC12312993](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12312993/)
- A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs covers app-based adherence interventions in cardiovascular disease (effect size not retrieved). — [UKE research portal record](https://fis.uke.de/portal/de/publications/mobile-apps-to-improve-medication-adherence-in-cardiovascular-disease-systematic-review-and-metaanalysis(0e8e0d34-76f7-40f7-b6b8-9595647db089)/export.html)
- MedISAFE-BP (Morawski et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2018): 2-arm RCT, 411 adults with poorly controlled hypertension; Medisafe users had a small improvement in self-reported adherence, but change in systolic BP did not differ (-10.6 mmHg intervention vs -10.1 mmHg control). Authors: benefit of stand-alone mHealth interventions on clinical outcomes "remains to be established". — [PubMed 29710289](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29710289/); [Pharmaceutical Journal](https://pharmaceutical-journal.com/article/news/adherence-app-has-no-impact-on-blood-pressure-control); [MobiHealthNews](https://mobihealthnews.com/content/study-app-improves-medication-adherence-no-change-systolic-blood-pressure-among-hypertension)
- Cochrane review (Nieuwlaat et al., 2014): 182 RCTs; only 17 at lowest risk of bias; those generally used complex multi-component interventions with tailored ongoing support from allied health professionals such as pharmacists (education, counselling, motivational interviewing or CBT, daily treatment support, sometimes family or peer support); only 5 of the 17 improved both adherence and clinical outcomes; "even the most effective interventions did not lead to large improvements". Conclusion: current methods are "mostly complex and not very effective"; calls for objective adherence measures and long-term, adequately powered trials. — [Cochrane CD000011](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000011_interventions-enhancing-medication-adherence); [National Elf Service commentary](https://nationalelfservice.net/treatment/medicine/we-dont-know-how-to-improve-medicine-adherence-says-new-cochrane-review)
- Self-report bias: MMAS-8 relies on self-report subject to recall and social desirability bias; in one study the scale classed 26% of patients as low adherence while therapeutic drug monitoring showed 51% non-adherent, giving MMAS-8 sensitivity of 26%. — [UT Health San Antonio scholars record, "Comparison of Morisky Medication Adherence Scale with therapeutic drug monitoring"](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-morisky-medication-adherence-scale-with-therapeutic/)
- Gamification fatigue: a scoping review found a lack of evidence that gamification sustains health behaviour change, with little consideration or measurement of long-term effects; most e-health applications and serious games yield only short-term engagement through extrinsic rewards. — [Stepanovic and Mettler, "Gamification applied for health promotion: does it really foster long-term engagement? A scoping review" (ECIS 2018)](https://aisel.aisnet.org/ecis2018_rp/50); [Univ. Lausanne repository](https://serval.unil.ch/notice/serval:BIB_50A0EC5E3795)
- Gamification effect decay: a meta-analysis of gamified physical-activity interventions found the effect persists after the programme ends (so not purely novelty) but at follow-up averaging 14 weeks it is "more volatile" with a "very small to small" effect. — [University XP summary of "Does gamification improve physical activity? A systematic review and meta-analysis"](https://www.universityxp.com/research/2021/5/4/does-gamification-improve-physical-activity-a-systematic-review-and-meta-analysis); [DOAJ record, J Med Internet Res, Jan 2022](https://doaj.org/article/9754f1fd9dd84266b5e5a30336858ad8)
- Equity: most adults with outpatient visits at a large urban health system did not use the patient portal; initiation was lower for racial and ethnic minorities, people of lower socioeconomic status and those without neighbourhood broadband. — [PMC4799429](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4799429)
- Equity, age: 45% of adults aged 65-80 and 40% of those aged 50-80 had no patient portal account (University of Michigan National Poll on Healthy Aging, as reported). — [TechTarget / PatientEngagementHIT, "4 SDOH Driving Health Disparities in Patient Portal Use"](https://www.techtarget.com/patientengagement/news/366585043/4-SDOH-Driving-Health-Disparities-in-Patient-Portal-Use)
- Equity, inpatient portal: African American patients used the portal less than White patients, and patients aged 60-69 and 70+ used it less than those aged 18-29. — [PMC6259664](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6259664)

### Inferences
- MediBridge's medication component is entirely self-reported taps, the weakest class of adherence measurement in the literature; a patient can raise their Care Score by tapping "taken" without taking anything. Because the score is patient-only today the incentive to game is small, but it would grow if the doctor or an admin (or an insurer-style reward) saw it.
- A score that rewards recent checkups and completed follow-ups will systematically score lower for patients with cost, distance, literacy or connectivity barriers. Presented to a doctor without context, it risks labelling disadvantaged patients as "non-compliant". This is a design consideration specifically relevant to an Indian deployment.
- The Cochrane finding implies the highest-value use of the score is not the number itself but triggering human follow-up (doctor or pharmacist outreach, barrier-specific counselling), which is what the effective trials had in common.
- Evaluators asking "does it work?" can be answered honestly: reminders and tracking have modest evidence for self-reported adherence, no robust evidence for clinical outcomes, and the Care Score itself is untested.

### Gaps
- Pooled effect sizes, heterogeneity (I-squared) and risk-of-bias detail for the JMIR 2025 and the 2021 cardiovascular meta-analyses were not retrieved (full text blocked).
- I found no study evaluating a composite patient-facing "care/engagement score" as an intervention; the evidence above concerns apps and reminders generally.
- No India-specific trial of an adherence app was retrieved.
- The racial-disparity figure reported by the search tool as a "40.4% difference, p = 0.004" could not be checked against the paper's definition and is deliberately not reproduced as a finding.
- Authorship of the gamification meta-analysis (JMIR 2022) was not confirmed from the primary article.

## 5. What do "unified health timeline" features look like in Apple Health Records, ABHA app, MyChart, Eka Care, Google Health Connect?

### Takeaway
A chronological, multi-source health record view has existed since at least 2018 (Apple Health Records) and is government infrastructure in India (ABHA / ABDM health lockers with consent-based sharing). MediBridge's Health Timeline is a single-provider version of an established pattern and is weaker than these on interoperability.

### Cited Findings
- Apple Health Records (announced January 24, 2018): records from multiple institutions are aggregated into one view and "organized into a clear, easy to understand timeline view" covering allergies, conditions, immunizations, lab results, medications, procedures and vitals, with notifications when data is updated; built on FHIR; stored in HealthKit, encrypted and protected by the iPhone passcode. — [Apple Newsroom, 2018](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2018/01/apple-announces-effortless-solution-bringing-health-records-to-iPhone/); [Apple WWDC 2018 session 706](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2018/706/)
- Apple Health Records directory: about 300 healthcare organizations, labs and clinics with over 6,500 US locations (figure from an older article, date not confirmed; the current number is likely much higher). — [HospiMedica](https://mobile.hospimedica.com/health-it/articles/294778669/apple-opens-iphone-ehr-feature-to-us-healthcare-organizations.html)
- ABHA app (National Health Authority, India): create an ABHA address; link and view records from ABDM-compliant facilities (lab reports, prescriptions, vaccination certificates); self-upload physical records to ABDM-compliant health lockers; granular consent controlling who can access which records and for how long, with revocation; share records with a doctor, facility or health programme; scan a facility QR code for quick registration; locate ABDM facilities. — [ABDM, ABHA App document (PDF)](https://abdm.gov.in:8081/uploads/ABHA_App_8413f7c295.pdf); [UX4G case study on ABHA](https://www.ux4g.gov.in/case-studies/ux4g-abha.php); [India Med Today on revamped ABHA app](https://indiamedtoday.com/nha-launches-revamped-abha-mobile-application/)
- A health-locker ecosystem exists around ABDM (article titled "223 health locker apps promoted under ABDM", February 2022). — [MediaNama, 2022](https://www.medianama.com/2022/02/223-health-locker-apps-promoted-under-abdm/)
- Eka Care stores, shares and gives access to ABHA-linked medical reports, prescriptions and diagnoses alongside vitals and medication trackers, syncing with Health Connect and Google Fit. — [Apple App Store, Eka](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1561621558)
- MyChart gives patients online access to their personal medical record plus a Health Reminders section for due preventive care. — [Premier Health, 2013](https://www.premierhealth.com/news-and-events/news/2013/01/29/dr-davis-shares-how-mychart-electronic-medical-records-play-a-role-in-preventive-care)

### Inferences
- MediBridge's timeline merges three types (appointments, records, medications) from one application's own database. Apple and ABHA merge many clinical types across many institutions using FHIR / ABDM standards. On breadth and interoperability MediBridge is behind, not ahead.
- What the incumbent timelines generally do not include is patient behaviour events (individual doses taken or missed, follow-ups completed) interleaved with clinical events. A timeline that visually correlates adherence dips with visits or symptom records would be a modest but real differentiation.
- Adopting FHIR-shaped data or ABDM-style time-boxed, revocable consent for doctor access would be the most credible way for a student project to align with the Indian national architecture.

### Gaps

## 6. What evidence-based adherence interventions exist that are not commonly implemented in apps?

### Takeaway
The interventions with the best trial evidence are human-delivered, tailored and multi-component (pharmacist or allied-health counselling, motivational interviewing, barrier-specific support, family/peer support, teach-back), plus validated barrier questionnaires; consumer apps mostly implement only reminders and logs. This is the clearest space for MediBridge to add something evaluators would recognise as grounded in evidence.

### Cited Findings
- In the Cochrane review, the lowest-risk-of-bias trials generally used complex interventions that tried "to overcome barriers to adherence by means of tailored ongoing support from allied health professionals such as pharmacists", with intense education, counselling (including motivational interviewing or cognitive behavioural therapy) or daily treatment support, and sometimes support from family or peers. — [Cochrane CD000011](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD000011_interventions-enhancing-medication-adherence)
- Teach-back: a systematic review found teach-back effective for improving health literacy in chronic disease, superior to traditional education for knowledge retention and self-care behaviours, and effective for medication adherence in diabetes, hypertension and heart failure; outcomes improved included disease-specific knowledge, adherence, self-efficacy and inhaler technique. A stated gap is practical strategies for implementing it widely in real-world settings. — [PMC12680514, "Empowering patients through effective communication: the teach-back method as a tool for health literacy"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12680514/); [Monash University record, "The effectiveness of the teach-back method on adherence and self-management in health education for people with chronic disease: a systematic review"](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/the-effectiveness-of-the-teach-back-method-on-adherence-and-self-/)
- MARS-5 (Medication Adherence Report Scale, 5 items): performed well on several psychometric indicators and "shows promise as an effective self-report tool" across conditions. — [Chan et al., Br J Clin Pharmacol (UCL repository PDF)](https://discovery-pp.ucl.ac.uk/10085255/1/Chan_bcp.14193.pdf)
- MMAS-8 (Morisky): widely used 8-item self-report scale and the outcome measure in several app meta-analyses, but with low sensitivity against drug-level monitoring (26% in one study) and limited construct validity in gout. — [UT Health San Antonio record](https://scholars.uthscsa.edu/en/publications/comparison-of-morisky-medication-adherence-scale-with-therapeutic/); [CORE, "Utility of the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale in gout"](https://core.ac.uk/works/121500064)
- Caregiver involvement has vendor-reported supporting data (40% of non-adherent Medisafe users became adherent after adding a Medfriend); not independently verified. — [Medisafe](https://medisafe.com/education-resources/the-impact-of-medisafes-medfriend-caregiver-feature-on-adherence)
- Comprehensive Medication Review completion (pharmacist-led review of all of a patient's medicines) is itself a Medicare Star Ratings measure ("MTM Program Completion Rate for CMR"), indicating regulators treat structured medication review as a quality marker alongside adherence. — [PQA Medicare Part D page](https://www.pqaalliance.org/medicare-part-d)
- Default/pended orders plus pre- and post-visit nudges for overdue care are under trial in Epic, a "make the right action the default" approach rather than a score. — [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07636187](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07636187)

### Inferences
- Candidate evidence-grounded additions that existing consumer apps rarely offer and that fit MediBridge's patient+doctor data model: (a) capture a reason when a dose is marked skipped or missed (forgot, side effect, cost, ran out, felt better) and surface the pattern to the doctor, turning the Care Score from a grade into a diagnostic of barriers; (b) a short digital teach-back after a prescription is issued (patient restates how and when to take each medicine; doctor sees mismatches); (c) a brief MARS-5-style barrier questionnaire at intervals (check licensing: the Morisky scale is known to be aggressively licensed, so do not copy MMAS items); (d) a medication-reconciliation step at each appointment comparing the doctor's prescription list with the patient's self-added medicines, which MediBridge uniquely holds side by side; (e) doctor-triggered outreach when the score's follow-up component drops.
- These reposition the feature from "another gamified score" (crowded, weak evidence) to "a doctor-visible adherence-barrier loop" (the mechanism the Cochrane evidence favours), which is the strongest answer to the evaluators' overlap criticism.

### Gaps
- Shared decision making and medication reconciliation were not specifically searched; no effect sizes or sources retrieved for either. The Morisky licensing concern is UNVERIFIED background knowledge and should be checked before being stated in the report.
- No quantitative effect size for teach-back on adherence was retrieved.
- No evidence retrieved on whether collecting skip reasons in an app improves outcomes; items (a)-(e) above are design inferences, not tested interventions.
